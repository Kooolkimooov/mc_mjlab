# Joint residual with motion authority

## LocomanipMotionAction

**Current:** The `locomanip_motion` task adds one action after the existing
joint-position residual. Zero or negative values keep normal speed; `0.5`
requests half speed; values at least `0.95` request a hold. Intermediate
nonzero speeds are bounded below by 0.1. The bridge dispatches the resulting
scalar through `Locomanip::setMotionScale` only with fresh bilateral Hold
and healthy controller/worker status. Each reset row returns to normal speed.

The actor and critic observe the dispatched scale, controller-applied scale,
motion state, post-hold resume limit, accepted/filtered intent and request delay.
Joint magnitude/rate penalties describe joint
offsets. Motion diagnostics are separate because speed scale is dimensionless.
The action and observation changes have distinct full task IDs and experiment
names, generated by the naming helper. The original joint task remains usable
with its existing action interface.

**Re-measure if:** the action mapping, phase gating, callback implementation,
controller tuning or payload changes.

**History:** Added on 2026-10-14 after the joint residual was found to have no
explicit command for reducing the controller's walking/cart motion demand.

## setMotionScale

**Current:** The callback lives in the external LocomanipController source,
alongside its `ManipManager` implementation. Installed controller revision
`2a6fec7` removes the post-hold speed cap; the callback began in `91570d6`.
`motionScale` reports the
controller's scale and `motionState` reports 0 for following the route, 1 for
braking and 2 for holding. Normal speed does not change the original trajectory.
Scale changes of at least 0.1 initiate a stop/replan transition during Hold.
The callback rejects nonfinite or out-of-range scales; near-zero requests mean
hold. It applies to waypoint motion, not the controller's separate velocity mode.

The transition clears future footsteps while retaining an active swing.
`MotionStopTrajectory` wraps a copy of the current object trajectory and
continuously reduces its time derivative to zero over at least 0.5 seconds,
waiting for the retained step to finish. The cart reference then remains fixed.
The FSM keeps both hands in Hold instead of proceeding to release.
When a positive scale is requested, the remaining waypoint goals are restored
with longer durations and footsteps are regenerated to follow them. Partial
segments use a duration bound that preserves peak velocity and acceleration
when the interpolation restarts from rest. A one-second
lead-in lets the controller prepare the next step. The final cart goal is retained.
After an explicit hold, the positive request selects the resumed speed, including
full speed. The `motionScaleLimit` callback has been removed. The mjlab action,
observations and probe use the remaining callbacks and check that the actual
resumed scale equals the request. GUI controls and log entries added in `c7c9682`
remain disabled by the training YAML's GUI/log settings.

A held episode still ends at the configured wall-clock cap. Holding does not
set `Locomanip::complete`, and an incomplete episode is not a safe completion.
A full training run must evaluate both failure and completion/displacement;
surviving by remaining stationary is not evidence of finishing the task.

**Re-measure if:** the footstep queue cancellation, object interpolation,
FSM completion guard or timing changes. The controller module and companion
library hashes bind new checkpoints and authority reports to this implementation.

**History:** Revisions through `fb867cb` capped resumed motion at half speed until
episode reset after an observed loaded failure on full-speed resume. Those
measurements and both 200-iteration pilots used that older behavior. Their saved
controller hashes differ from the current installed binaries; they cannot be
resumed or evaluated as the same controller under the new installation.
The previous `clearWaypointQueue()` utility noted a discontinuous
object-velocity change. This control path retains the incoming reference velocity
and applies the chain rule while braking, rather than replacing a moving reference
with a zero-velocity interpolation in one step.

## score_braking

**Current:** `scripts/probe_locomanip_motion.py --reward-audit --registered-cap`
adds a fourth arm that holds until timeout. Every trace includes the actual
timestep reward, its already evaluated weighted reward rates, bilateral Hold,
and termination/truncation flags before reset. No reward callable is evaluated
twice. `scripts/audit_locomanip_braking.py` scores the counterfactual reward
`reward - cost * (1 - applied_scale) * step_dt` during bilateral Hold. The
controller's applied scale includes the half-speed limit after a hold even
when the policy requests normal speed.

Returns start at the earliest measured intervention decision in the matched
batch and use the task's `gamma=0.997`, `step_dt=0.02`. They include terminal
failure rewards. Each trace must contain consecutive policy steps from reset
through a terminal observation, and each timestep reward must agree with its
captured terms. Untriggered interventions and worker failures are rejected.
Both discounted and undiscounted returns and the coefficient that makes each
arm equal the baseline are saved, with the source run's controller provenance.

These are measured finite-episode rewards under fixed actions, not learned
PPO values. The task marks completion and timeout as truncations; their
unknown critic bootstrap values are explicitly excluded. Indefinite holding
at timeout remains incomplete regardless of return. A coefficient above 1
makes the maximum full-hold reward rate negative, but that bound alone does
not establish that successful braking outscores a delayed fall.

```sh
uv run python scripts/probe_locomanip_motion.py --mass 550 --registered-cap \
  --reward-audit --output logs/locomanip/braking-test/mass-550
uv run python scripts/audit_locomanip_braking.py \
  logs/locomanip/braking-test/mass-550 \
  --output logs/locomanip/braking-test/audit.json
```

**Re-measure if:** the reward weights, action mapping, phase gate, discount,
timestep, controller, mass, bias setting or episode cap changes.

**History:** The initial proposed coefficient was 1.1, compared with the
maximum positive DCM reward rate of 1. The configured fall weight of -200
becomes a single -4 reward at the 0.02-second policy timestep.

## Braking reward audit on 2026-10-14

**Current:** Do not use the proposed linear coefficient 1.1 for training.
It reverses the desired ordering in both batches with a falling baseline:
falling earns more discounted reward than either successful intervention.
The diagnostic initially left the training rewards unchanged; the following
pilot section records the subsequently authorized training coefficient.

arm and batch, 19 episodes total. Four-arm batches at 100, 525, 550 and
575 kg include indefinite Hold; another 550 kg batch uses the original

`91570d618902930d78671d607a58f912b427933a`, companion hash
`a621349832ab9f9d2bb5c160f68cad1fcb86d2ff0992343296f88dc4c3820915`.
Reports, complete terminal traces and scored returns are under
`logs/locomanip/braking-reward-2026-10-14/`; `audit.json` combines all batches.

| batch | baseline | half speed | hold then half-speed resume |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 100 kg, four arms | completed 49.28 s | completed 82.44 s | completed 86.42 s |
| 525 kg, four arms | completed 49.28 s | completed 82.38 s | completed 86.42 s |
| 550 kg, four arms | completed 49.26 s | completed 82.38 s | completed 86.38 s |
| 550 kg, three arms | fell 36.16 s | completed 82.40 s | completed 86.42 s |

| batch and arm | no cost | coefficient 0.075 | coefficient 1.1 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 550 kg, three-arm baseline fall | 1.3478 | 1.3478 | 1.3478 |
| 550 kg, three-arm half speed | 1.7642 | 1.5140 | -1.9049 |
| 550 kg, three-arm hold/resume | 1.7263 | 1.3710 | -3.4850 |
| 575 kg, baseline fall | 0.1935 | 0.1935 | 0.1935 |
| 575 kg, half speed | 1.9648 | 1.7154 | -1.6933 |
| 575 kg, hold/resume | 1.9194 | 1.5641 | -3.2919 |

For these measured schedules, preferring normal completion whenever the
baseline is safe and both successful interventions whenever it falls
requires approximately `0.07272 < cost < 0.07989`. The lower bound comes
from normal completion versus unnecessary hold/resume at 100 kg. The
upper bound comes from successful hold/resume versus falling in the
three-arm 550 kg batch. At 0.075 the respective return margins are only
0.0108 and 0.0232. This coefficient was fitted on these same observations;
the narrow interval is not a validated training setting.

A coefficient above 1 guarantees a negative full-hold reward rate but is
incompatible with the measured rescue upper bound. A smaller coefficient
can offset the observed extra return from unnecessary braking without
making every held timestep negative. Neither comparison proves that a
policy cannot exploit a different trigger or prolonged partial slowdown.

**Re-measure if:** selecting a training coefficient, changing the reward
shape, or changing any input listed under `score_braking`. Repeat near the
transition with training encoder bias enabled before accepting a fitted
coefficient; these are diagnostic schedules, not policy qualification.

**History:** The four-arm 550 kg baseline completed, so the original
three-arm layout and a 575 kg batch were added to obtain actual falling
comparators. Both fall-containing probes exit nonzero because the original
plumbing checks require a safe, completed baseline; their terminal traces
remain valid measured failure cases for the reward audit.

## BRAKING_START_COST

**Current:** The revised task charges 0.22 reward per actual controller braking
start, plus the smaller `BRAKING_COST=0.03` ongoing rate. Actual held time
also pays `HOLDING_COST=0.05` per second. `ManipManager` owns a
monotonic `motionStopCount` that resets with the episode. Its scalar callback
is collected through the usual bridge. The action accumulates positive count
deltas over every native collect in a policy step, excluding stale or failed
rows. `process_actions` clears the step accumulator, and partial resets clear
only the selected rows and their count baselines.

`mdp.braking_start` divides that count by `env.step_dt`, so the reward manager's
time integration charges exactly 0.22 for one start at either a 0.02- or
0.01-second policy timestep. `braking_starts` records the native episode total
with `reduce="last"`. It does not infer edges from sampled transition states,
which can miss short intervals between events.

The reward audit removes any already captured `braking`, `braking_start` and `holding`
terms before scoring counterfactual costs. New traces use native events; old
traces use explicitly labelled sampled edges. `--event-costs` compares startup
costs alongside the ongoing rate. On the old recorded schedules, rate 0.03
and event cost 0.22 preserve the intended completion/failure ordering, but the
smallest rescue margin remains about 0.016 reward. This is an experimental
setting fitted to those observations, not an independently validated result.
The final controller audit found that those two costs alone preferred
indefinite Hold to hold/resume at 550 kg, leading to the separate held-time
cost recorded below.

**Re-measure if:** event accounting, reset timing, policy timestep, rewards,
controller or mass/bias conditions change. Recheck actual event charges and
successful rescue returns before increasing the cost.

**History:** Useful constant slowdown and hold/resume traces each started
braking once. The stalled learned policy started braking 73–81 times at
100/550 kg. An event cost targets this repeated startup behavior without
charging the same high rate throughout a legitimate slow route. A derivative
cost is deferred because a large squared rate cost also charges an abrupt
emergency request.

## MOTION_FILTER_GETTER

**Current:** Controller revision `2a6fec7` retains the `fb867cb` request filter with
a 0.25-second time constant, a 0.1 deadband, 0.05 target bins and 0.5 seconds
of stable intent. A restarted route is protected through its one-second
lead-in and two subsequent seconds of motion before accepting another ordinary
change. Explicit zero requests bypass filtering and protection immediately;
the existing continuous stop remains in place. The post-hold half-speed cap
was removed in `2a6fec7`.
Release from an explicit hold bypasses the ordinary filter delay, then resumes
with the same protected lead-in and motion interval.

`motionRequestScale`, `motionFilteredScale` and `motionRequestDelay` expose
accepted intent, filtered intent and remaining request delay to the actor and
critic. This changes the observation interface and controller binaries, so the
old pilot checkpoint remains tied to its previous inputs. The next pilot must
start fresh. Project controller YAMLs remain minimal.

The native `motion_stop` CTest checks normal requests under burst noise and
PPO-like Gaussian exploration, sustained half-speed acceptance, restart
protection, immediate hold, reset and the existing interpolation bounds.
`probe_locomanip_motion.py --jitter --encoder-bias --registered-cap` adds
0.1/0.05 standard-deviation motion requests and explicitly verifies native
event accounting. Constant slowdown, deliberate hold/resume and noisy requests
share matched payload and encoder-bias inputs within each live batch.

**Re-measure if:** filter timing, target bins, deadband, restart protection,
exploration, policy frequency or controller waypoint timing changes.

**History:** The unfiltered interface treated each sufficiently different speed
request as a complete stop/replan and could repeatedly cancel its own restart
delay. This protection addresses that controller behavior independently of
the policy's incentive to avoid unnecessary braking.
The first filtering build, `34bbcc8`, also filtered a released explicit hold
and extended the requested four seconds to about 5.42 seconds. Revision
`fb867cb` removes that unintended extension.

## HOLDING_COST

**Current:** Actual held time during bilateral Hold costs an additional 0.10
reward per second. Together with the slowdown rate, a full hold pays 0.13
per second after its startup cost; travelling at half speed pays 0.015 per
second. The term reads the controller's held state, not an action threshold,
and excludes inactive hand phases. Normal travel incurs neither cost.

The held-time rate was retuned together with DCM and failure weights against
the uncapped controller. These are diagnostic reward-ordering checks, not
qualification of a learned policy.

**Re-measure if:** the held-state definition, timing, controller or rewards
change. A derivative penalty remains deferred; an emergency stop should
remain available without a large squared request-rate charge.

**History:** The event cost targets repeated startup but is already paid
when deciding whether to remain held. A separate duration cost addresses
that later decision without increasing the cost of a legitimate slow route.
The initial held-time cost was 0.05, giving total full-hold cost 0.08/s.
On the final recorded 550 kg trajectories, startup cost 0.22 plus slowdown
rate 0.03 gave hold/resume 1.3025 reward and indefinite holding 1.4153. Adding
that 0.05 held-time cost changed those to 1.1623 and 1.1070 respectively,
while the failing baseline earned -0.2955. Those measurements used `fb867cb`;
the same profile did not preserve the ordering with the later uncapped controller.

## motion_decision_period_s

**Current:** Motion selections are latched for 2.0 s, or 100 policy steps
at the registered 0.02 s period. Joint residuals continue at 50 Hz. The
period must be an integer number of policy steps. Only fresh, healthy,
bilateral Hold permits a selection. Leaving that phase clears the latch
and clock; reentry selects immediately. Partial resets clear selected rows.

The uncorrupted `motion_decision` observation group contains eligibility,
remaining clock divided by the interval and the latched raw request.
Both actor and critic receive it. Clock observations keep the augmented
environment state observable; eligibility is read before action processing.
The native filter and restart protection are unchanged.

Two seconds allows the approximately 1.25 s acceptance time measured for
a sustained half-speed request. It also means a newly needed policy command
can wait up to two seconds for the next decision. Native explicit hold still
bypasses its filter when selected; it does not override the policy clock.
This latency is a tradeoff to test near failures, not a safety guarantee.
